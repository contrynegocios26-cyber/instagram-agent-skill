# B2B / Vertical AI businesses with proven revenue traction in the US (2025-2026)

> Researcher note on source quality (read first). Notes compiled 2026-11-12. Almost every figure below came from web-search result summaries, not from pages I opened and read in full; the one primary page I tried to fetch (menlovc.com) was blocked by the session network. Many of the URLs are aggregators (Sacra, Enterprise DNA, multiples.vc, ai2.work, fourweekmba, pulse.bot) that restate press reports. Each number is labelled as **company-reported**, **press-reported**, or **estimate**. Where the search summary did not tie a claim to one specific URL, I cite the most plausible URL from that result set; the report writer should treat single-source aggregator numbers as directional. "ARR" at AI companies is usually an annualized run-rate (last month x 12), often includes usage/consumption revenue, and is never audited.

## 1. Which B2B AI categories have the clearest revenue traction in the US?

### Takeaway
Ranked by proven revenue, the clearest traction is in (1) AI coding tools (multiple companies above $500M-$4B run-rate), (2) AI customer-support agents (Sierra ~$200M, Decagon ~$100M, Intercom Fin ~$100M), (3) healthcare documentation/clinical AI (Abridge, OpenEvidence, Ambience; healthcare is ~43% of all vertical AI spend), (4) legal AI (Harvey $300-400M+, Legora $200M+, EvenUp $150M contracted) and (5) voice-AI infrastructure and vertical voice agents (ElevenLabs $500-600M, Retell ~$60M, EliseAI $200M+, Avoca, HappyRobot, Salient). SMB-facing verticals (AI receptionists, dental/vet, restaurants, insurance, construction, accounting) show strong funding but little disclosed revenue, and AI SDR is the clearest documented disappointment.

### Inferences
(These are my own reasoning on target customer, ROI, GTM and build difficulty; they are not sourced facts.)

| Category | Who pays | Why they pay (ROI) | GTM motion | Build difficulty | US-specific dependence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coding tools | Developers, then enterprise engineering orgs | Engineer salaries are the largest cost line; productivity gain is easy to justify | Bottom-up self-serve, then enterprise contracts | Very high: needs frontier-model access, huge inference spend, competes with model labs | Low (global), but capital needs make replication unrealistic |
| Support agents (enterprise) | Large consumer brands | Replace $3-8 human-handled tickets with ~$1-1.50 resolutions | Top-down enterprise sales with forward-deployed engineers | Medium-high: integrations, guardrails, evals, voice | Medium: ROI scales with agent wages; Brazil's lower wages shrink savings but WhatsApp volume is huge |
| SMB AI receptionist | Local service businesses | Captures missed calls/after-hours leads; replaces $300+/month answering services | Self-serve, agencies/resellers, vertical software partnerships | Low: thin layer on Vapi/Retell/ElevenLabs; differentiation is distribution and integrations | Medium: US SMBs are phone-first; Brazil is WhatsApp-first, so channel must change |
| Home-services voice AI | HVAC/plumbing/electrical contractors (often PE-backed) | Each booked job is worth hundreds to thousands of dollars | Sales-led, integration with ServiceTitan-type systems | Medium: value is in deep CRM/dispatch integration | High: depends on large, software-equipped contractors and high ticket sizes |

## 4. Which pricing models are winning?

### Takeaway
Pricing is moving away from pure per-seat toward usage and outcomes: per-resolution for support agents ($0.99-$1.50), per-minute for voice ($0.07-$0.30 all-in at the platform layer), flat monthly bundles for SMB receptionists ($49-$300), per-seat still dominant in legal, coding and scribes, and consumption/credits layered on top in coding, search and sales data.

### Cited Findings
- Per-resolution: Intercom Fin $0.99 per resolution (50/month minimum on third-party helpdesks) — [fin.ai pricing](https://www.fin.ai/pricing)
- Outcome-based enterprise contracts: Sierra ~$1.50 per resolution, ~$150K/year entry; Decagon ~$50K platform fee plus per-conversation usage (single unverified blog) — [DragApp](https://www.dragapp.com/blog/decagon-vs-sierra/)
- Intercom's president has publicly credited outcome-based pricing for Fin's performance — [GTMnow](https://gtmnow.com/how-intercom-built-the-highest-performing-ai-agent-on-the-market-using-outcome-based-pricing-with-archana-agrawal-president-at-intercom/)
- Per-minute voice: Vapi $0.05 (orchestration only), Retell $0.07-$0.31, Bland $0.11-$0.14, Synthflow from $0.09; all-in $0.07-$0.30 — [Morph](https://www.morphllm.com/comparisons/vapi-vs-retell-vs-bland-vs-synthflow); [beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/voice-ai-per-minute-pricing-vapi-retell-elevenlabs-bland-cost-per-resolved-call)
- Flat SMB subscription with minute/call caps: $49-$149 (Rosie), $59-$79 unlimited (Goodcall), $65-$99 (My AI Front Desk), $95-$292 (Smith.ai hybrid) — [Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/how-much-does-an-ai-receptionist-cost); [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/best-ai-for-receptionists)
- Per-seat: self-serve scribes ~$99/clinician/month; enterprise scribes $300-700/month; Epic's native scribe reportedly ~$80/provider/month — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/freed-vs-heidi-health/); [OrbDoc](https://orbdoc-website.pages.dev/compare/emr-sponsored-vs-independent-ai-scribes)
- Consumption inside "ARR": part of Glean's ARR is a consumption run-rate; Clay's revenue is credit-based — [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/glean-triples-to-300m-arr-on-token-cost-pitch); [LeadGenius](https://www.leadgenius.com/resources/the-clay-bubble-why-100m-arr-isnt-what-it-seems-and-why-ai-might-be-the-pin-that-pops-it)
- Advertising: OpenEvidence is free to physicians and monetizes through pharma advertising — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/openevidence-vs-doximity/)
- Outcome/subcontractor-style contracts are emerging in construction robotics/AI (Monumental, TerraFirma, Gritt) — [Bricks & Bytes](https://bricks-bytes.beehiiv.com/p/hardware-robotics-contech-money-what-changed)
- Becoming the service provider instead of selling software: Harper operates as the insurance broker and earns commissions — [Emergence](https://www.emcap.com/thoughts/harper-building-the-ai-native-insurance-brokerage-every-business-deserves)

### Inferences
- Outcome pricing wins where the unit of work is countable and has a known human cost (a ticket, a call, a booked job, a demand letter). Per-seat survives where the buyer is a high-paid professional (lawyer, doctor, engineer).
- For Brazil, per-outcome and per-conversation pricing transfers well, but the price ceiling is set by local labor cost: a $0.99-$1.50 resolution is cheap against a US agent and much less so against a Brazilian call-center agent.

### Gaps
- No verified enterprise price sheets for Sierra, Decagon, Harvey, Abridge, EliseAI, Avoca, Salient or HappyRobot.
- No data on Harvey/Legora per-seat prices in the results.

## 5. Which categories failed or are commoditizing?

### Takeaway
The clearest failure is the autonomous AI SDR (11x's reported 70-80% three-month churn and disputed ARR). The clearest commoditization is the AI scribe (Epic's ~$80/month native scribe and Doximity's free scribe against $300-700 incumbents) and the generic voice-agent platform layer (per-minute price competition). Thin wrappers face structurally lower margins (about 50-60% versus 80-90% for SaaS) and high consumer-style churn.

### Cited Findings
- 11x: TechCrunch (Mar 2025) reported 70-80% churn within ~3 months and ARR claims that former employees disputed (claimed ~$14M versus ~$3M); 11x responded that it reports contracted ARR to its board — [Adrata](https://adrata.com/articles/the-11x-lesson); [AI SDR post-mortem](https://deathtocoldemails.pages.dev/ai-sdr-post-mortem/)
- Commentary that most AI SDR pilots are failing and being replaced by human-in-the-loop tools — [subagentgtm](https://subagentgtm-com.pages.dev/blog/why-ai-sdrs-are-failing/)
- Scribes: Epic's native scribe at roughly $80/provider/month (described as rumored in some coverage) pressures vendors charging $300-700; Doximity launched a free scribe; analysts call note-taking a commoditized feature — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/ai-and-machine-learning/how-epics-ai-moves-could-shake-health-tech-market); [Wachter Substack](https://robertwachter.substack.com/p/platforms); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/8f5f6f02-3165-4096-b9df-05641aa0c052/)
- Home-services voice: ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro and Jobber now bundle their own AI call agents — [stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/blog/ai-receptionist-for-home-services-2026)
- Margins: Bessemer's Feb 2026 pricing playbook puts AI product gross margins at 50-60% versus 80-90% for SaaS; ICONIQ data put the average AI product gross margin near 52% (up from 41% in 2024) and inference at ~23% of revenue for scaling AI B2B companies (all cited second-hand) — [Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/13/llm-api-cost-cogs-ai-wrapper-gross-margin-guide)
- Coding/app-builder margins: Replit -14% to 23% gross margin during 2025; Lovable 36% in May 2025 — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/replit-at-253m-arr-growing-2352-yoy/); [Sacra](https://www.sacra.com/c/lovable)
- Churn: ChartMogul finds consumer-leaning AI wrappers churn more, while AI products are not inherently high-churn; a widely repeated "65% churn in 90 days" figure for wrappers could not be traced to a primary dataset — [ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-retention-the-ai-churn-wave/); [AI Eating the World](https://www.aieatingtheworld.com/articles/ai-wrapper-business-why-most-fail-2026)
- Counter-view: a16z argues that questioning AI app margins is overdone — [a16z](https://a16z.com/questioning-margins-is-a-boring-cliche)
- Mercor's "revenue" is gross with 60-70% paid out to contractors, illustrating how headline AI revenue can overstate software economics — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/mercor/)

### Inferences
- The pattern: a product commoditizes when (a) the system-of-record owner can bundle it (Epic for scribes, ServiceTitan for trades, Zendesk/Salesforce for support) or (b) the model labs ship it directly (coding assistants, generic chat/writing). Survivors own workflow depth, proprietary data, compliance, or the outcome itself.
- For a Brazilian replicator the scribe and SMB-receptionist stories carry the same warning: the note or the phone answer is cheap to build and will be priced toward zero; the durable revenue is in integrations with local systems (e.g., local ERPs/clinic software, WhatsApp, Pix, local tax and billing rules), which US incumbents do not serve.
- Fully autonomous outbound sales is the weakest model to copy; its US evidence is negative.

### Gaps
- No Jasper or other 2023-era copywriting-wrapper revenue decline data was found in this session.
- Claims such as "80% of wrappers fail by end-2026" (attributed to CB Insights/Gartner) and "only 3% of AI startups survive two years" (attributed to McKinsey) appear only on secondary blogs with unclear sourcing and were excluded.
- No churn or net-retention data for the vertical voice companies beyond Salient's own no-churn claim.
